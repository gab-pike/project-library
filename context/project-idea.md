# Personal Project Library

## The Problem

Multipassionate people often have many projects, hobbies, and areas of interest operating at the same time.

A person might spend several weeks building a web application, shift their attention to a landscaping project, begin editing a video, reorganize part of their home, and then return to the original software project several months later.

The problem is not necessarily starting projects or having too many interests. The problem is **maintaining continuity across those interests over time**.

When a project is set aside, much of its context is eventually lost:

* What had already been accomplished?
* What decisions were made, and why?
* What resources or references were useful?
* What still needed to be done?
* What problems were encountered?
* How was something configured or built?
* What was the original vision for the project?
* Where exactly did the project leave off?

Returning to an old project can therefore feel less like continuing something and more like reconstructing it.

The same problem exists with completed projects. Someone may solve a complicated problem, develop a useful process, or learn an entirely new skill while completing a project, only to forget much of what they learned after moving on to another interest.

Traditional task managers are primarily designed around **what needs to be done next**. Note-taking applications primarily store information. Project-management platforms are often designed around teams, businesses, deadlines, and productivity workflows.

What is missing is a personal system designed around **remembering, documenting, pausing, resuming, and learning from projects across many different areas of life.**

---

## The Solution

The Personal Project Library would be a web application designed specifically for managing and documenting **personal projects across many different hobbies and interests**.

Rather than treating every interest as something that must be constantly active or completed as quickly as possible, the application would recognize that personal projects naturally move through periods of activity, inactivity, experimentation, and renewed interest.

Each project would function as its own organized workspace and historical record.

Instead of having project information scattered across notes apps, browser bookmarks, folders, photographs, task lists, documents, and memory, everything related to a project could be collected in one place.

A user could open an old project months or even years later and quickly understand:

**What was I doing?
Why was I doing it this way?
What have I already accomplished?
What did I learn?
And what should I do next?**

---

## A Library for Projects, Not Just Tasks

At the center of the application would be a personal **Project Library**.

Projects could belong to customizable categories such as:

* Software Development
* Photography
* Videography
* Home Improvement
* Landscaping & Gardening
* Art
* Making & Fabrication
* Research
* Personal Organization
* or any other interest the user wants to create.

The categories would not impose a particular workflow. Their purpose would simply be to make a large and diverse collection of projects easy to navigate.

Projects could also be assigned priorities such as **High, Medium, or Low Priority**, allowing users to distinguish between something they are actively trying to accomplish and something they are simply interested in returning to eventually.

---

## The Project Workspace

Each project would contain its own structured workspace where the user could collect the information necessary to both manage the project now and understand it later.

A project could include:

### Overview

A description of the project, its purpose, and the original idea behind it.

### Goals

What the user ultimately wants to accomplish and what a successful outcome looks like.

### Tasks and To-Do Lists

Actionable items required to move the project forward.

### Important Dates and Schedule

Deadlines, milestones, appointments, launch dates, seasonal considerations, or other time-sensitive information.

### Progress Updates

A chronological record of what has happened during the project.

These updates would help create a history of the project rather than simply showing its current state.

### Project Notes

Projects often accumulate much more information than can comfortably fit into one notes field.

Instead, each project could contain multiple individual notes or documents organized by subject.

For example, a software project might contain separate notes for:

* Research
* UI ideas
* Bugs
* Docker configuration
* Database questions
* Things I learned
* Future features

A landscaping project might instead contain:

* Plant research
* Measurements
* Soil notes
* Materials
* Design ideas
* Seasonal observations

The goal would be to make project knowledge easy to navigate rather than creating one enormous running document.

### Resources and External Links

Useful websites, tutorials, products, documentation, inspiration, references, or other external resources could be stored directly with the project where they are most relevant.

### Assets

Projects could also contain or reference files such as:

* Images
* Documents
* PDFs
* Videos
* Design files
* Screenshots
* Reference material

Assets could potentially be uploaded directly or embedded/linked from external sources.

## Designed for Projects That Get Paused

One of the application's most important ideas would be recognizing that **putting a project down does not mean abandoning it**.

A user should be able to pause a project without losing their place.

Before stepping away, they could record:

* what they most recently accomplished,
* what they were working on,
* any unresolved problems,
* and what they intended to do next.

When they eventually return, the application could make that information immediately visible.

Instead of asking:

> "Where was I?"

the application should help answer that question for them.

---

## Reusing What Has Already Been Learned

Personal projects frequently repeat.

Someone who builds one Dockerized web application may eventually build another. Someone who creates one garden bed may later create three more. A photographer may repeat a similar publishing workflow for multiple photo series.

The application would therefore allow users to **duplicate an existing project** and use it as the foundation for a new one.

Rather than rebuilding the project's structure, research, checklists, and workflow from memory, users could reuse what already worked and modify it for the new project.

Over time, the user's project history becomes a personal knowledge base.

---

## The Project Idea Inbox

Not every idea needs to immediately become a project.

The application would include a simple place for capturing **project ideas** without requiring the user to plan or organize them yet.

An idea might simply be:

> Build a greenhouse monitoring dashboard.

> Photograph abandoned rural churches.

> Redesign the basement workshop.

> Learn how to build a browser extension.

These ideas could live in an idea library until the user decides one is worth developing into a full project.

This creates a separation between **having an idea** and **committing to a project**, reducing the pressure to immediately act on every interesting thought.

---

## Completing and Preserving Projects

When a project is finished, it would not simply disappear into an archive.

Eventually, the application could help generate a **Project Summary** documenting:

* what the project was,
* what was accomplished,
* important decisions,
* major challenges,
* useful resources,
* lessons learned,
* and the final result.

The completed project would become something the user could return to later as a reference.

Over time, the application would effectively build a searchable record of the user's creative work, experiments, knowledge, and accomplishments.

---

## Designed for Multipassionate and Neurodivergent Minds

The application would be designed around the reality that some people do not work on one interest at a time.

Their attention may naturally rotate between different subjects, sometimes rapidly and sometimes over months or years.

Instead of treating this behavior as a failure of organization, the system would be designed around it.

The goal would not be to force users to become relentlessly productive or finish every idea they have.

The goal would be to **reduce the cognitive cost of having many interests.**

The software would provide enough structure to preserve context without requiring excessive organization or maintenance.

It should make it easy to:

**Capture an idea.
Start a project.
Document what happens.
Put it down.
Find it again.
Remember where you were.
Continue when the interest returns.
And preserve what you learned when it is finished.**

---

## Core Concept

**A personal project library for multipassionate people that preserves the context, knowledge, progress, and history of everything they create.**

Rather than simply helping people manage what they need to do, the application helps them remember **what they have done, how they did it, and where to begin when they return.**
